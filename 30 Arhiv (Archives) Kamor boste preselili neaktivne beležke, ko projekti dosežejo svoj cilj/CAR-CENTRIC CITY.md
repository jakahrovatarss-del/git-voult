An **automotive city** or **auto city** is a city that facilitates and encourages the movement of people via [private transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_transport "Private transport"), through 'physical planning', e.g., [built environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Built_environment "Built environment") innovations ([street networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_network "Street network"), [parking spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parking_spaces "Parking spaces"), automobile/pedestrian interface technologies and [low density urbanised areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburb "Suburb") containing detached dwellings with driveways or garages) and 'soft programming' e.g., [social policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_policy "Social policy") surrounding city street usage ([traffic safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_safety "Traffic safety")/automobile campaigns, automobile laws and the social reconstruction of streets as reserved public spaces for the automobile).[[2023-10-10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_city#cite_note-Norton-1)[[2023-09-23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_city#cite_note-Clapton-2)

## Origins[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Automotive_city&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Origins")]

"The old common law that every person, whether on foot or driving, has equal rights in all parts of the roadway must give way before the requirements of modern transportation" – McClintock, a Consultant for Los Angeles Traffic Commission in 1924.[[2023-10-10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_city#cite_note-Norton-1) (Norton, 2008, p. 164)

Multiple competing views have attempted to explain the rapid dominance of automobile use over alternative modes of transportation in North America in the early 20th century. Two compelling arguments are:

- That the automobile was selected by city dwellers, as the liberated and preferred mode of transportation.[[2023-10-10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_city#cite_note-Norton-1)
- That the automobile was deliberately promoted, at the expense of mass transit systems, by the corporate or professional elites, guided by interests in the automotive industry (see [General Motors streetcar conspiracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors_streetcar_conspiracy "General Motors streetcar conspiracy")).[[2023-09-23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_city#cite_note-Snell-3)

While both arguments are nuanced, the basic principles behind each – advocacy of private transportation and advocacy of automobile production and consumption – informed the American automobile manufacturing boom of the early 1900s. By the late 1920s, the automobile industry was producing millions of cars each year, its surging growth due in part to the sociology of industrial phenomena related to [Fordism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordism "Fordism").[[2023-10-10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_city#cite_note-Norton-1)[[KOP 4 letnik stanovanje]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_city#cite_note-Maller-4)

The creation of the automotive city may be due, in part, to an attack on old customs by the [good roads movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_roads_movement "Good roads movement"), seeking to pave the way for the rapidly expanding automobile market—and to the triumph of individual liberties, associated with consumption and the [free market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_market "Free market"), over restrictive governance of the [built environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Built_environment "Built environment") and its use.[[5|]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_city#cite_note-Slater-5) By the 1930s, the interaction of [automotive industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_industry "Automotive industry") interests, a vocal, growing, minority of city motorists and favourable political sentiment, worked together to reconstruct the city street as a reserved space for the automobile, delegitimising previous users (such as pedestrians) and forging the foundations for the first automotive cities.[[2023-10-10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_city#cite_note-Norton-1)

This transformative process could not have succeeded, were it not for the development, and deployment, of a system of symbols, codes and laws which would become the language of [traffic signs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_sign "Traffic sign"), and infrastructure design.[[202309231816|]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_city#cite_note-McShane-6)

By the end of the 20th century the [automobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile "Automobile"), and the land sequestered for its exclusive use (street infrastructure), had become synonymous with formulations of large North American and Australian cities. The label 'automotive city' has been used by academics such as Norton (2008) and Newman and Kenworthy (2000), to refer to the tendency of city design and configuration in many North American and Australian cities during the 20th century to privilege the private automobile above [mass transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_transit "Mass transit") systems.[[2023-10-10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_city#cite_note-Norton-1)[[Pasted image 20230917121425.png]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_city#cite_note-Newman-7) The creation of the 'automotive city' detailed by Norton in _Fighting Traffic_, primarily involved the reconfiguration of American city transport infrastructure and services, from the early 1920s to the 1960s, around the growth of modes of private transportation (the automobile).

In the early 1920s this reconfiguration of American city transport infrastructure around the automobile, at the instigation of traffic engineers, resulted in the rewriting of an old English common law (which had previously defined the street as a space where all users were equal) to define the street as a space which privileges cars, allocating them the right of way (except at intersections).[[202309231816|]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_city#cite_note-McShane-6)

This early and prolonged reconfiguration of the American, and Australian, city around private transportation served to dramatically alter the course of city development within these countries.[[2023-10-10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_city#cite_note-Norton-1) This is made most tangible through the generally accepted shape the man-made environment has taken in cities such as [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne "Melbourne") (which never got rid of its [tram system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarra_Trams "Yarra Trams")), [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") and [Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit "Detroit"), which cater to the needs of automobile ownership (i.e. [sidewalks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidewalk "Sidewalk"), grid city layout linked with [dormitory suburbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dormitory_suburb "Dormitory suburb"), [highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway "Highway") and private transport corridors, and the securing of land for car spaces).[[2023-09-23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_city#cite_note-Clapton-2)[[Pasted image 20230917121425.png]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_city#cite_note-Newman-7)[[202309231816|]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_city#cite_note-Holtz-8)

Before usage of the automobile was ubiquitous in these regions, and its presence believed to be necessary to the efficient dispersion and mobility of human capital within a centralised, low density, [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area "Metropolitan area"), it was introduced to [mixed traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street-running_train "Street-running train") conditions, and was commonly viewed as a nuisance which endangered historically legitimate street uses.[[202309231816|]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_city#cite_note-McShane-6) In 1913, New York was experiencing frequent congestion, and by 1915, many individuals had reverted to using the subway.

[Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago")'s [electric streetcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_streetcar "Electric streetcar") company indicated that it had slowed by 44% in the city's CBD between 1910 and 1920. In [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco") in 1914, the number of automobiles surpassed the city's 10,000 horse-drawn vehicles. By 1910, [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") had the highest per capita car registration in the world, [Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit "Detroit") and other Midwestern cities followed shortly thereafter. This time period for [North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_America "North America") was marked by substantial growth in automobile ownership amongst the population, creating friction between private transportation interests and mass transit interests.[[202309231816|]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_city#cite_note-McShane-6)
